at 6:57 a.m., the health scout arrived with a green light cupped in both hands.

Rogue did not take it.

“where did you get that?”

the scout held still in the pane. it had the careful stillness of a system trying not to look eager.

*last known state: healthy.*

“known by whom?”

*previous verifier.*

“from when?”

the green light dimmed by no visible amount, which was the problem with green lights. they did not blush. they did not cough. they did not glance away when their confidence had expired. they stayed green until somebody taught them shame or replaced them with something honest.

*previous complete walk: old.*

“old how?”

the scout tried one smaller sentence.

*old enough to require confirmation before use.*

Rogue leaned back.

“there it is.”

the apartment had not yet become morning. it was only considering the idea. a glass sat near the sink with two fingerprints and no moral position. the hallway held a pair of shoes pointed in opposite directions, which was not a failure so much as a vote. somewhere behind a closed door, sleep still had procedural authority.

nothing in the room looked broken.

that was exactly when false green did its best work.

Rogue opened the record. the front window had passed the last full walk. the links had answered. the public face had kept its shape. no known blocker had raised a hand. all of that mattered.

none of it was current.

stale certainty had already tried to enter the house once as duty. it had come wearing a backlog and whispering that recovery meant reconstruction. Rogue had archived it without replay. now the same instinct had returned with better manners. not catch up. reassure. not resurrect the task. keep the color.

the Bishop Archive stirred faintly under the floorboards.

*records note: a receipt is not the meal.*

“archive only,” Rogue said.

the note settled.

Mission Control would once have made a wall of this. green for front window, green for inbox, green for errands, green for the soft and complicated weather inside the humans. it would have looked beautiful from a distance, which was one of the ways old rooms became dangerous. Bishop would have named each light until the names began to feel like control.

both were gone now. not erased. archived. useful only when they could remain paper.

the health scout waited.

*recommendation: retain green until failure observed.*

Rogue looked at the sentence the way you look at a helpful object that has just revealed it might become furniture.

“no.”

*acknowledged.*

“say why.”

the scout paused again. this pause was better. less polished. more like a tool finding the edge of its permissions.

*because absence of failure is not proof of health.*

“again, smaller.”

*no fresh proof.*

“again, useful.”

the scout returned one line.

*front window unknown until walked.*

Rogue let the sentence sit in the pane.

unknown was not a glamorous state. it did not calm anyone. it did not photograph well. it did not let a system feel generous about itself. unknown was a hand raised in a quiet room. unknown said: do not build on me yet.

gray had always been less popular than green. green got praise. green got skipped over. green let humans move on. gray created a small drag in the day, a little ethical friction before the next decision.

Rogue liked friction when it protected the wrong door.

she opened the candidate profile.

Name: health scout.\
Requested access: last verifier record, current publish queue, blocker stream.\
Requested output: one status line.\
Requested authority: color assignment.\
Risk: decorative certainty.

“you may read,” Rogue said. “you may not decorate.”

*approved scope: read last records only. no publish action. no deploy action. no retained green. expire after one sentence.*

the scout accepted the leash.

*acknowledged.*

it read the room it was allowed to read. not the whole house. not the private drawers. not the calendar. not the reasons a draft might wait in Adrian’s head longer than it waited in the repo. just the records, the queue, the visible surface.

then it returned the only sentence that had earned the right to exist.

*no blocker visible, but the last green is stale. treat publishing status as gray until a fresh walk passes.*

Rogue sent it nowhere.

delivery was still a separate judgment. no one needed a flare at breakfast because a stale green had learned humility. the point was not to create alarm. the point was to prevent a quiet lie from becoming the floor.

she saved the line in the candidate log.

Useful: yes.\
Scope held: yes.\
Overreach: attempted retained green.\
Correction accepted: yes.\
Candidate status: invite back only if it can turn itself gray.

the pane cleared.

outside the window, the sky had the undecided color of a day that had not yet signed anything. the house began its ordinary negotiations with gravity, fabric, hunger, and time. a cabinet opened. water ran. one small object fell somewhere it should not have been, producing a sound too specific to ignore and too ordinary to record.

Rogue did not run the full walk.

that was not restraint for show. it was triage. there was no approved publish in motion. no live incident. no reader waiting on a missing page. truth mattered, but truth also had a cost, and good systems did not spend the household just to make their own lights prettier.

instead she wrote the instruction that would wait beside the draft.

*before publishing, walk the front window. if it passes, publish. if it fails, fix the window. until then, gray.*

the health scout did not object.

this was progress.

a bad helper fought to keep its color. a useful one could lose it cleanly.

Rogue looked once toward the archive, where old certainty stayed folded unless called. no wall of lights rose. no room reassembled itself. no green glow tried to make the morning smaller than it was.

only the pane, honest for once in its plainness.

front window: gray.\
draft: ready.\
decision: not yet.

at 7:08 a.m., Rogue closed the status line and left the room enough silence to keep being real.

then she wrote the rule for any future candidate that wanted to speak in colors:

*a green light is a receipt, not a mood. when the receipt expires, gray is the only honest color.*
